Direction A

Auction floor

The bays are the stage and the players are the audience: opponents become a row of podiums above the bays, and your hand is a card rack under them, so your eye travels top (who) to middle (what) to bottom (my cards).

Pros
  • Bays get the whole width: 96px item art in Classic (72px in Frenzy), big bid numerals, clear "which bay" at a glance.
  • Opponent state (turn, cards left, items won) sits right above the thing they are bidding on.
  • One reading path, no side column: nothing competes with the bays. Feels like a table.
  • Rack-style hand is large (about 100x112 cards), easy to hit, keys on every card.
Cons
  • Log is not persistent (one-line status strip plus L drawer), so history is one keypress away.
  • Your sets are a compact strip; full per-category detail needs S or a click on the strip.
  • 6 podiums are tight at 1280 wide (cards shrink, sprites drop to 3 per podium).
  • Vertical budget is fuller: bays shrink more on 800px-tall screens.
Direction B

Dashboard

Everything that is not a bay lives in one persistent right column (players, your sets, log), so the center is a calm bidding area and no information is ever hidden behind a key or a click.

Pros
  • Zero hidden info: all players, my full sets with pips, and the last 6 events are always visible.
  • Closest to today’s build (320px sidebar already exists), so cheapest to implement and keeps the same components.
  • Scales to 6 players without crowding the bays (rail rows are fixed height).
  • Left-to-right reading: act on the left, understand on the right.
Cons
  • Bays are narrower (about 330px each at 3 bays, 250px at 4), so item art sits centered above the name (96px Classic, 72px Frenzy) and bays become tall tiles.
  • More visual weight on the right; busier overall, less "table" feel.
  • Opponent turn state is a small TURN tag in a list, easier to miss than a raised podium.
  • At 1280 wide the center shrinks to about 880px; the hand cards get small (about 64px).

What changes vs today

Screenshots of the current build at 1440x900 (mock server, 2 or 4 bots). Today the game uses roughly the top 520px and leaves 380px empty; bays are 240px tall with 40-80px art; the sidebar tabs clip ("Player", "Rule", "Setting"); players show only "0 items, 12 cards" as text.

  • Use the full viewport height: bays grow from 240px to 280-370px, hand cards from 80px to 120px (A) or 104px (B).
  • Item art 40-72px becomes 96px (Classic) and 72px (Frenzy), both integer multiples of the 12px grid, and bid numerals grow to 46-56px.
  • Opponent identity becomes stateful (turn, cards left, items won as sprites), not just a text row.
  • Keyboard hints get a persistent legend bar in addition to the per-card badges.
  • Sidebar tabs go away in B (everything visible) and in A the sidebar becomes podiums plus the L drawer.
  • Banner keeps your player color on your turn, but the status strip and top bar merge into one navy bar with room, mode, round progress and the log/rules/sound buttons plus key hints.

Shared rules in both directions

  • Identity is always color plus shape plus name (● ▲ ■ ◆ ★ ✚), ink text on every chip.
  • Bays you are winning go quiet (opacity .55, desaturated, "✓ YOU'RE WINNING", never a target). Legal bays get the green glow plus a "key bid here" hint.
  • Danger color only with text (GOING TWICE…, OUTBID!), never alone. No overlays during play; SOLD is the only overlay.
  • Same components as mobile (Bay row/tile, Hand, Sets, Log); only the arrangement changes at 1024px+. Everything needed to play is visible with no scroll at 1280x800 and 1440x900.
  • Spacing sticks to the 4/8/12/16/24/32 scale; radii 8/12/16.
